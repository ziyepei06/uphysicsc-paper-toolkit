# Reading List: How to Use the 74 Gold Medal Papers

Read the eight papers below first (10–20 minutes each), picked by what you need. The full index follows.

## Eight close reads

### 1. `2020/2020-GM-A-354.pdf` — the structure textbook (23 pages)
1 Notations → 2 Introduction → 3 Theory → 4 Method → 5 Results (5.1–5.4, one per trajectory leg) → 6 Discussion and Conclusion → 6.1 Limitations → 7 Appendix.
**Learn**: Results subsections map one-to-one onto the questions; each opens with the answer, then the figure. Limitations get their own subsection.

### 2. `2023/2023-GM-A-131.pdf` — summary and validation (33 pages, 18 figures, 10 refs)
The summary runs: situation → assumptions → model → validation against Felix Baumgartner's 39 km jump → numbers.
**Learn**: validate against one real data point; put a specific number in the summary.

### 3. `2017/2017-GM-A-699.pdf` — how to write strengths and weaknesses (28 pages)
5.1 Strengths / 5.2 Weaknesses, itemised, each tied to a model component (PSO convergence, ideal-sail assumption).
**Learn**: not "the model has error", but "neglecting X biases Y by about Z%".

### 4. `2025/2025-GM-B-188.pdf` — layered modelling at scale (69 pages, 36 figures, 25 numbered subsections)
1 Introduction (1.1 Background / 1.2 Problem Restatement) → 2 Notations → 3 Assumptions (grouped 3.1–3.3) → 4 Modelling (4.1 no-drag, 4.2 atmosphere, 4.3 drag, …) adding one effect per subsection.
**Learn**: group assumptions by theme; add complexity layer by layer and say what each layer changed.

### 5. `2022/2022-GM-B-471.pdf` — figure density, upper bound (44 pages, 33 figures)
**Learn**: every figure must serve a claim. Conversely, fewer than eight figures usually means missing parameter sweeps or error analysis.

### 6. `2019/2019-GM-A-289.pdf` — engineering-scheme argument (28 pages, 18 figures, 13 refs)
2 Background (radiation types, biological effects, existing concepts) → 5 Scheme (5.1–5.8: principle, material, structure, simulation, thermal, magnetic field, mass estimate).
**Learn**: for a design problem, organise as principle → components → system → mass/cost accounting, then give a feasibility verdict.

### 7. `2021/2021-GM-B-216.pdf` — short and complete (20 pages, 11 figures)
**Learn**: 20 pages can win gold; what matters is answering every question and justifying every assumption. Page count is not scored.

### 8. `2016/2016-GM-A-331.pdf` — short paper, risk framing (16 pages, 1 figure, 12 refs)
Compares two space-based nuclear-waste disposal schemes (solar deorbiting vs asteroid-belt storage) and includes a probability/failure analysis.
**Learn**: 16 pages and one figure can win gold — the argument chain matters more than figure count; risk problems need a probability model, not just qualitative discussion.
